The rule

From big to small

The bigger and more general the time, the higher it is. The smaller and more exact, the lower it goes.

Look closer

In, on, at — with examples

Read the examples aloud. Then make one sentence about you for each box.

inBIG · GENERAL
  • yearsI was born in 2008.
  • decades · centuriesRock was huge in the 1970s.
  • seasonsWe play outside in the summer.
  • monthsThe school concert is in May.
  • parts of the dayI practice in the evening.
onMORE SPECIFIC
  • daysWe have band practice on Tuesdays.
  • datesThe show is on March 6.
  • one-day holidaysWe watch fireworks on July 4th.
  • special daysI got a guitar on my birthday.
  • day + part of daySee you on Monday morning!
atEXACT
  • clock timesThe concert starts at 8 p.m.
  • noon · midnightThe party ended at midnight.
  • meal timesWe talked about it at lunch.
  • holiday seasonsMy cousins visit us at Christmas.
  • nightI write songs at night.

Tricky ones

Parts of the day

in the morning, in the afternoon, in the evening
but at night

One day or the season?

on Christmas Day one day
at Christmas the whole holiday time

Day + part of day

on Friday night, on Sunday morning
in Sunday morning

The weekend

on the weekend American
at the weekend British

No in, on or at!

today · tomorrow · yesterday · this / next / last / every + time
I have a test next Friday. We saw them last summer. I practice every evening. on next Friday
